A home page that
does not break

The live home page is close. Its bones are right: a full-bleed hero, a ruled mono metadata layer, a packed six-frame strip, one black band to close on. It has one critical defect and two structural ones. The H1 breaks PORTRAITUR / E on a phone because overflow-wrap:anywhere was used to cure horizontal scroll by letting any word break anywhere. The right fix is to size the type to the measure, not to license the break. This document shows the defect, the fix, and eight more changes that give the page a section rhythm and a closing band worth the scroll.

04

The arithmetic behind change 01

measured, not estimated

Every number in this table came out of the CDP measurement pass in audit-v2/, not out of a stylesheet guess. The live H1 is clamp(48px, 9.6vw, 132px), so on any phone narrower than 500px the 48px floor wins and the vw term is inert — which is why the type never shrinks on small screens and the word always breaks.

The constant

7.7em is the measured width of "PORTRAITURE" in Archivo Black at the site's own tracking. That single number is what makes the cap correct rather than a fudge factor — and it is already written down in editorial.css:230, where a different measurement (6.3em, taken before the tracking changed) is used for the desktop cap. Both numbers are load-bearing and should live in one place.

Why not just shrink it

Setting a smaller fixed font-size fixes 393px and breaks 320px and 430px, because the relationship between the word and the column is not linear in viewport width. Sizing from the measure is the only version that is right at every width without a breakpoint per device.

The honest limitation

This document renders in a system grotesque, which is roughly 15% narrower than Archivo Black — so the line you see here is not quite full. That is a property of the mockup, not of the fix. On the live site with Archivo Black the 46.4px line fills the 354px column almost exactly, which is the intent.

05

Change list

11 changes · S / M / L

S = a few CSS lines, no structural change. M = a markup change in src/pages.ts plus CSS. L = a change that touches layout logic or data. Total for items 1–11 excluding item 6: one small half-day, most of it in the stylesheet.

  1. 01

    Size the H1 to its measure, not to the viewport

    S

    Replace the clamp(48px, 9.6vw, 132px) floor with min(clamp(34px, 9.6vw, 132px), (100cqi - 2 * var(--pad)) / 7.7) on a container-query context, and give the heading two explicit line spans so the break after TIMELESS is designed.

    Why: the 48px floor is the whole bug. It overrides the vw term on every phone, so the word is always 14–87px too wide. Sizing from the measure makes the type correct at every width with no breakpoint, and the two spans turn an accident into a decision. Yields 46.4px at 393px, 36.9px at 320px, and 132px unchanged on desktop.

  2. 02

    Delete overflow-wrap: anywhere and pin the no-break values

    S

    Remove editorial.css:237-239. Set overflow-wrap:normal; word-break:keep-all; hyphens:none on the hero heading so the failure mode for any future font swap is a clipped word rather than a split one. Add one assertion to the existing harness: at 320 / 375 / 393 / 430px, the H1's longest line must not exceed the column.

    Why: anywhere was a band-aid — it cured horizontal scroll by licensing a break anywhere in the page, not just in this one heading. It is the wrong tool at every scale, and while it is in place the same trick will eventually reappear somewhere else. The assertion means the bug cannot come back silently on a new device.

  3. 03

    One control group in the header, anchored right

    S

    Below 900px: wordmark flush left; then Instagram, WhatsApp, a 1px hairline, and a 40px bordered burger — flush right, in that order. The burger stops being a centred grid cell.

    Why: the burger is the only navigation on a phone, so it belongs at the thumb end and should be a visible 44px target, not three bare bars stranded between two other things. The hairline between it and the socials is the site's own visual vocabulary separating secondary from primary — no new device, no new colour.

  4. 04

    Give the mono register one pattern and reuse it

    M

    The hairline + label-left + meta-right row that already exists as .hstrip__head becomes .reg, and every section on the page uses it: the hero register, the strip header, and the band header.

    Why: right now the strip announces itself one way and the black band another, so the page reads as a stack of unrelated blocks instead of a document. One repeated pattern, applied three times, is what makes a single-column page feel designed — and it is the mono layer doing structural work rather than decorating.

  5. 05

    Bring the subject tags back on phones

    S

    .hero__meta-tags and .hero__meta-cue are currently display:none below 640px. Move the tags into the hero's right-hand register cell and stack the year above the place.

    Why: "Portraits · Couples · Families" is the fastest possible answer to "what does this person do", and it is being hidden on exactly the devices with the least room for persuasion. The two-line register fits the width and gives the hero a proper bottom edge instead of a single thin strip of text.

  6. 06

    Name the hero's focal point

    S

    object-position: 68% 22% on the hero image, replacing the default centre. Document the face coordinates of every future hero photo next to its heroPhoto pin in config.ts.

    Why: 27-img-0297 has the subject's face at roughly 68% across and 15% down. Cropped to a 0.64 aspect box from the centre, the frame is fine; cropped the wrong way it is a leg. heroPhoto is a pin anyone can change, so the focal point has to travel with the pin. A full-bleed hero must crop; the rule is that we choose where, not that we don't.

  7. 07

    Kill the orphan link and the 88px of nothing

    S

    Move "See the full portfolio" from a centred line floating 14px under the last frame to a right-aligned action on a closing hairline, and tighten the strip-to-band gap.

    Why: as it stands the strip has no bottom edge — a caption-sized link, then 88px of white, then a hard black block. Giving the link a rule to sit on closes the section and lets the band arrive as a change of pace rather than a jump cut.

  8. 08

    Rebuild the band as a contact bar, not a second hero

    M

    Same words, same price, same data. Three changes of emphasis: the display line drops to clamp(28px, 7.4vw, 64px) with the same no-break guarantee; the price gets its own step in the scale below the heading instead of sitting directly under it; and the band's header becomes a .reg like every other section.

    Why: the band currently opens with a 48px stacked display line that is nearly as large as the hero's, so the page has two moments competing for maximum scale and the first one loses. Holding the band a full step down gives the hero back its job and makes the band read as a closing statement. Same content, better hierarchy — no copy invented.

  9. 09

    Make the contact lines real tap targets

    S

    Replace the .hcta__foot link cluster with labelled rows: a 82px mono key column, the value beside it, min-height:46px each, hairlines between. Add the studio's hours as a fourth, non-link row, and pad the band's bottom by var(--sab) so the home indicator does not sit on the last row.

    Why: 10px letterspaced mono links at the bottom of a black band are the smallest and least tappable thing on the page, and they carry the two conversions the whole site exists to produce. On a phone they are also the only affordance — the burger menu has to be opened to get anywhere else. Labeled rows also make the block scannable instead of reading as an anonymous string of links.

  10. 10

    Whole frames everywhere except the hero

    S

    Strip cells keep width:100%; height:auto with the aspect ratio from data. No object-fit on any of the six. State it in HOME-LOCKED.md alongside the locked count so it is not "tidied" later.

    Why: cropping a photographer's own work to make a grid tidy is the fastest way to lose them — a portrait cropped to a square loses the gesture that made it worth commissioning. The packer already balances the columns; the frames do not need to be cut to match.

  11. 11

    Nothing repaints on rotate

    S

    Because change 01 is pure CSS, there is no resize handler to write and nothing to reflow on orientationchange. State that as a constraint in the stylesheet so the next person does not add a JS sizer to "fix" the heading.

    Why: a resize listener on iOS white-flashes the page and jumps to the top on every rotation, which on a photography site reads as a crash. The tempting JS fix for a type-fitting problem is the exact thing that causes it. Solving 01 in CSS makes 11 free.

Deliberately not changed

  • Six frames, two columns. HOME-LOCKED.md locks homeGalleryCount: 6 and the column count, and the comment in config.ts:233-245 explains why 6 is the only count that divides evenly at every viewport. Not reopened here.
  • No site-wide footer. An earlier footer was reverted. The band still ends the page; change 09 just makes its contact lines usable instead of replacing them with a new component.
  • No colour, anywhere. Including the price. "From R1,200" in white on black stays exactly as it is.
  • No new copy. Every word in the proposed render already exists in src/config.ts — the year, the place, the three subjects, the tier names, the deposit note, the studio hours. Change 05 simply stops hiding copy that was already there.
