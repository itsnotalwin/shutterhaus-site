01 — Now · as shipped

What the client actually sees today

Reproduced from src/pages-more.ts with the shipped cascade intact, at 393 px (--pad: 18px, --head: 92px, .pkgrow single column). The bugs below are rendered faithfully, not corrected — the invisible type is genuinely near-black on near-black. Scroll the screen; the numbered pins are anchored to what they point at.

  • 1
    Critical · 1.05:1

    The word “Investment” is invisible, and so are the CTA and its rule

    .invest is #0d0d0d with color:#fff. But .eyebrow--inv has no rule anywhere in the stylesheet, so .eyebrow (editorial.css:18) applies and paints color: var(--ink) = #111 on #0d0d0d.

    The same cascade reaches further than the reported bug. .cta (editorial.css:89) also sets color: var(--ink) and .cta::before { background: var(--ink) }, and neither has a .invest override — the hero gets one (editorial.css:268–269), the band does not. So “Let’s create together” and its em-dash are also black on black. Three invisible elements, one cause: a class name that was never written.

    Root cause: editorial.css:599 already defines .invest__label for this exact slot and nothing uses it.

  • 2
    Critical · 0 treatment

    .pkg--pop is a no-op. The recommended tier is identical to the other three.

    pages-more.ts:58 emits pkg pkg--pop for Essential (popular: true, config.ts:149), and .pkg--pop has no rule in editorial.css or styles.css. No border, no badge, no lift, no weight change. The client is asked to choose between four identical-looking cards on the one page built to make a decision. The pricing route already solved this — .tier--pop inverts to background: var(--ink); color: #fff (styles.css:847). The services route simply never got it.

  • 3
    Brand violation

    Every package photo is centre-cropped by object-fit: cover into a 4:3 box

    .pkg__fig is aspect-ratio: 4/3 with img { height:100%; object-fit:cover } (editorial.css:556–558). Every source frame is portrait 2:3, so a 4:3 window throws away roughly 55% of the picture and re-frames every face the same way, blind. Alwin's rule is explicit: never object-fit: cover on photographs — focal point and object-position only. Contact page does the same thing (.ct__fig, editorial.css:620).

  • 4
    Structure

    No price index. Four vertical cards is 2100 px of scrolling to answer “what does it cost?”

    .pkgrow collapses to one column at 760 px, so name, description, five bullets, price and CTA stack four times over. The price — the only number the client came for — is buried ~600 px down, below two paragraphs of feature copy, and appears four separate times at four different scroll depths. Compare-then-choose is impossible in one pass.

  • 5
    Conversion

    The page ends on legal text, and the last CTA above the fold is 2000 px up

    Order today: four “Book now” links → dark band → add-ons (6 rows) → booking terms (5 paragraphs) → end. The one real commitment moment, the deposit line, is the only visible thing in the band, at 12–14 px under a display heading that says nothing about money. After the band the client is asked to keep reading; nothing brings them back.

02 — Proposed · 393 px

Compare first. Then choose.

Same frame, same viewport, same brand. Four changes of substance: a price index above the cards so all four tiers are comparable in one screen, the recommended tier inverted to solid black (no colour, only value), the investment band rebuilt around the actual numbers, and a sticky thumb bar that keeps “Book now” in the bottom third of the screen for the whole page. Photographs sit at their natural 2:3 ratio — no crop, no object-fit.

  • 1
    Fixed

    The label is visible because the class name now matches the rule that already existed

    .invest__label (editorial.css:599) is not a recommendation — it is the correct rule, written and never used. Applying it is a one-token markup change, and white type is what it was always going to paint. The hairline above it is the only addition: the label needs a boundary, because on a black field a 10.5 px tracked-out word has nothing to align to.

    One line in pages-more.ts: class="eyebrow eyebrow--inv" → class="invest__label"
    One rule for the CTA the band never had: .invest .cta, .invest .cta::before { color: #fff; background: #fff }

  • 2
    Fixed

    “Most booked” reads in a quarter of a second — because the card inverts, not because it is coloured

    Black and white has one strong move available and it is value, so it is used once. The Essential card is solid #111 with white type, a white “MOST BOOKED” chip in the mono metadata position, and a filled white “Book Essential” button. Everything else stays hairline. The same treatment already exists on the pricing route, so this is consistency rather than novelty — the recommended tier now looks the same on both pages that sell the same four tiers.

    A ★ in the price index carries the signal upward to the one place the client actually decides.

  • 3
    Fixed

    The dark band now carries the number it exists to carry

    It says “Quality over quantity.” — a slogan — on the band where a client is deciding, with the price in no larger type than the add-on list. Now the band opens on a labelled ENTRY FROM R1,200 in the display face, and the deposit line is expanded to say what it covers: non-refundable, secures the date, balance before delivery. The CTA is a full-width white button, not a hairline link — it is the primary action of the page and it finally looks like one. The inversion itself is kept: it is the only black moment in the site and it earns its place by arriving exactly where the commitment happens.

  • 4
    Structure

    Price index above the cards; photographs at their natural ratio

    Four hairline rows, 56 px each, carrying name, session length, edited count and price — the whole comparison in 224 px, one screen, no scrolling. The recommended row is inverted in the index too, so the eye is caught before it starts reading. The cards below then answer “what do I get”, not “what does it cost”. Photography drops the 4:3 cover crop entirely: 96 px thumbnail at natural 2:3 on mobile, full column at natural 2:3 on desktop. Nothing is reframed, and four centre-cropped portraits stop pretending to be different photographs.

  • 5
    Structure

    Add-ons two-up, terms behind a disclosure, page ends on the ask

    Six add-on rows go from ~230 px to ~100 px as a two-column list with the prices in mono. Booking terms — five paragraphs, all of it conditional — collapse into a 52 px disclosure, so the page no longer ends on a R500 rescheduling fee. It ends on a closing CTA. And a sticky thumb bar holds the price and “Book now” in the bottom third for the entire page, so the client can act from wherever they are.

    Sticky bar is position: sticky; bottom: 0 with padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom). No resize listener anywhere — it never repaints on address-bar collapse, and orientation change is handled by the breakpoint alone.

03 — Proposed · 1280 px

The same page on a desktop

Not the mobile page stretched. The price index becomes a true five-column matrix — package, session, edited count, delivery, price — with the numbers on a shared baseline so they can be read as a column rather than as a list. The four cards sit on one row, and the recommended card is the only solid black object on the page. The terms disclosure opens into two columns.

04 — Change list

What ships, and what it costs

Effort is one developer-day-scale judgement: S = a selector and a value, M = a new block of markup plus CSS, L = a component that needs content, JS and QA across breakpoints.

01

Use the label class that already exists

One token in pages-more.ts:86: “eyebrow eyebrow--inv” → “invest__label”. The rule is at editorial.css:599 and has been correct since it was written; nothing has ever selected it. This is the whole fix for the reported critical bug and it is not a new line of CSS.

Add .invest .cta, .invest .cta::before { color: #fff } and .invest .cta::before { background: #fff }. The band’s CTA is black-on-black for the same reason the label is, and the brief did not catch it because the text is genuinely unreadable rather than merely quiet.

S2 selectors0 new deps
02

Give .pkg--pop the treatment the pricing route already has

Copy the inversion from styles.css:847 — solid #111 card, white type, white filled CTA — plus a “MOST BOOKED” chip in the mono metadata slot. Black and white, no colour, no lift, no shadow. Consistency argument: the same four tiers are sold on /pricing and /services, and today those two pages recommend the same package in two different visual languages, one of which is “nothing”.

M1 CSS block1 chip element
03

Insert the price index above the cards

A new .idx block between .shead and .pkgrow: four hairline rows, 56 px min, each an anchor to its card. Carries name, session length, edited count and price — the four facts that decide the purchase. The recommended row is inverted. On desktop the same markup becomes a five-column matrix with a shared baseline. This is the answer to “can I compare three tiers in ten seconds” — currently no, and it is not a typography problem.

M1 template loop1 breakpoint
04

Drop the 4:3 cover crop; let the photograph be its own ratio

Replace .pkg__fig { aspect-ratio: 4/3 } + img { height:100%; object-fit:cover } with the source ratio and width:100%; height:auto, plus width/height attributes on every <img> so the space is reserved before decode. All four sources are portrait 2:3; the mobile thumbnail is 96 px wide at that ratio, the desktop card is full column. No focal-point data needed, because nothing is cropped. Same change belongs on .ct__fig (editorial.css:620) — it crops 4:5 the same way.

Also worth noting: five images for the whole page today. Four uncropped portraits plus the band is the same count — this costs no extra weight.

S1 CSS change ×2 routes
05

Rebuild the investment band around the number

Add an “ENTRY FROM R1,200” display line in the mono-label slot, expand the deposit note to say what it covers (non-refundable, secures the date, balance before delivery), and promote the CTA to a full-width white button. Keep the #0d0d0d inversion — it is the only black moment in the site and it arrives exactly where the commitment happens, which is the right place to spend it. The hairline above the label gives the small type a boundary to align to.

Mcopy in config.ts
06

Sticky thumb bar with price and “Book now”

position: sticky; bottom: 0 inside the normal flow, padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom) so it clears the home indicator. Shown only after the price index scrolls out of view (IntersectionObserver), so it never covers the comparison it sits under. No resize listener is involved — a fixed/sticky element does not repaint when iOS collapses the address bar, which is the exact failure the “orientationchange, never resize” rule exists to prevent. Show/hide is a class toggle on scroll and orientationchange only.

Net effect: “Book now” is reachable from 100% of the page on a 393 px screen — today it is reachable from the top ~2000 px and then never again, because the page ends on a licensing paragraph.

L1 IO + scroll handleriOS QA
07

Two-up add-ons, terms in a disclosure, page ends on the ask

Add-ons to a two-column list (mobile) / three-column (desktop) with prices in the mono layer — ~230 px → ~100 px. Booking terms become a <details> with a 52 px summary row, so five paragraphs of conditional fine print stop being the last thing on the page. A closing CTA block ends the page on the ask rather than on a rescheduling fee. The terms are not hidden — one tap, and they are fully in the DOM for screen readers and crawlers.

Mno JS required
08

Per-card deposit figure

Each price now carries “R600 to book” in mono beneath it. A client deciding between R1,200 and R2,200 is partly deciding between R600 and R1,100 of money leaving their account this week, and today that number appears once, in 14 px body copy, in the middle of the page. Static text derived from the price — no new config field, no logic worth a build.

05 — Judgement

The four questions, answered

Can three — four — tiers be scanned in 10 seconds?

No. Not today.

Four cards, one column, ~525 px each. The name appears at four different scroll depths, the price at four more, and nothing aligns across cards, so the eye cannot compare down a column. The recommended tier is styled identically to the others, so the strongest signal the business has is not being sent at all.

After: the index puts all four decisions — duration, output, price — in 224 px on one screen, with the recommendation inverted. Ten seconds is generous; it is about four.

Does the dark band land, and is the deposit note clear?

It does not land. Two of its four elements are invisible.

The band is the right idea at the right place — the only inversion in the site, arriving at the commitment. But it currently says “Quality over quantity” in 24 px, shows the label and the CTA in #111 on #0d0d0d, and leaves one 14 px line as the only readable content. Nothing in it is about money.

After: it opens on “Entry from R1,200”, states what the deposit covers, and ends on a full-width button. The inversion now means “this is where you commit” instead of “this section is broken”.

Are add-ons and terms burying the CTA?

Yes — and the terms are the reason the page has no ending.

After the dark band, a client reads six add-on rows, five paragraphs of terms, and then the page ends. A R500 rescheduling fee is the last sentence they see. The only CTA in that stretch is 2,000 px higher up.

After: add-ons compress to ~100 px, terms sit behind one disclosure, the page ends on a CTA, and the sticky bar means the ask is never more than one thumb away regardless of where they are.

Is “Book now” repeated and thumb-reachable?

Repeated yes, reachable no.

Four cta--sm links plus the band link, all flush-left, all 44 px tall. They are reachable in the upper part of the screen only. The bottom third of the viewport — the natural thumb arc on a 852 px phone — contains a hairline link or nothing at all.

After: the bar sits in the bottom third for the whole page, carrying the entry price and a 44 px button, with env(safe-area-inset-bottom) clearance so the home indicator never covers it.
