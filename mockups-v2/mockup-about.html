Shutterhaus Visuals · About route · Redesign proposal v2

The credibility page is the thinnest page on the site.

What this is. The live #/about route measures 1206px tall at 393px with two images and one H1 — thinner than Contact, a quarter of Services. It is the only page on the site whose job is to answer "can I trust this person with my day", and it currently answers with three paragraphs that could belong to any photographer in any country.

The two findings that drive this design. First, the portrait in the right-hand column is not the photographer: aboutPage() takes photos[0], which is gallery/49-img-0131.jpg, a client's portrait, and presents it in the exact slot where a face of Alwin is expected. Second, the copy says nothing a client can act on — no occasion, no process, no turnaround, no price, no place. Both are fixed below, and the copy is written out in full rather than described.

Constraint held throughout. Black and white, zero colour, mono metadata layer intact and doing more work than before, no object-fit: cover on any photograph (natural ratios and focal points only), no numbers printed on images, flush-left with hairline rules, and the page reacts to orientationchange only — never to resize, which iOS fires on every address-bar collapse.

Change list

Effort: S under 30 min · M under 2 hrs · L half a day. Copy is written in full above; this table is the argument, not the content.

#ChangeWhyEffort
01 Drop photos[0] as the portrait. Replace it with a framed, captioned work frame plus an explicitly empty portrait slot. The actual defect photos[0] resolves to 49-img-0131.jpg — a client's portrait, presented in the exact slot a client reads as "the photographer". Every visitor sees a stranger and infers either a template or a lie. A framed frame captioned "AJ · Braamfontein · 2026" is honest, fills the same space, and proves craft at the same time. The slot below it states plainly that the face hasn't been shot, and ships the shot list that fixes it. Shipping the slot visibly empty is strictly better than shipping a stranger. S
02 Rewrite the H1. "Photography Is Poetry." → "I shoot real people, bad light, and long days." Duplicated and anonymous The current H1 is a verbatim repeat of the home-page eyebrow, so two routes open on identical words and the About route adds nothing. "Bad light" is the differentiator: it is the promise a photographer can keep, it tells a client how he works, and it is the opposite of the category cliché every other photographer uses. Stays 4 lines at 393px, still clamp()-driven at desktop. S
03 Open the body with who he is, not what he believes. Name, one-person operation, base. The first paragraph currently says nothing "Drawn to the raw, unfiltered beauty of real people" describes a sensibility, not a person, and could be pasted onto any site in the industry. A prospect needs three facts in the first screen: it's him, he's alone, he's reachable. "Not a studio, not a team, not a middleman" is also a positive claim — clients choose solo photographers for exactly that reason. S
04 Add What I shoot — and what it costs: four session blocks plus a plain add-on list. Answers "can he shoot my thing?" The one question a credibility page exists to answer, and the live page does not answer it even in hints — a visitor cannot tell whether matric farewells are in scope. Four named occasions do it in one scan. Every figure is lifted verbatim from SITE.pricing (R1,200 / R2,200 / R3,500 / R1,800, and the same six add-ons), which is what makes the block trustworthy rather than decorative: the price a client reads here is the price they will actually pay, and there is nothing to reconcile later. The add-on list printed openly does the same work — it removes the only reason a client hesitates, which is discovering an extra cost later. M
05 Add What happens after you enquire — five numbered steps from message to delivery. The biggest credibility gap on the page Nothing in the live page says what happens if you send an email. A prospect weighing a photographer is really asking "am I about to be ghosted?" Reply SLA, quote validity, deposit terms, cancellation protection and delivery window are the actual trust signals. Publishing the terms that are unfavourable to him — the R500 late-reschedule fee, the 50% non-refundable deposit, the separate commercial licence — is more persuasive than silence, because it reads as a real business rather than a page with nothing behind it. A prospect who finds those terms on the About page rather than after booking has been given a reason to trust the rest of what he wrote. M
06 Add a kit line (Canon EOS 4000D, 18–55mm) and the six-item ledger. Turns the budget into a style choice Naming the body and lens openly is a deliberate move, not a confession: a full-frame camera with a kit zoom, used in available light, is a coherent aesthetic argument and it explains the price. The ledger repeats the promises in scannable form for the visitor who reads labels and skips prose — a real segment, and the reason the mono layer exists. S
07 Invert the close into a dark full-bleed band with two CTAs and a stated response promise. The page currently ends in nothing "Let's create together" is a line of copy, not an action — no method, no expectation, no fallback. The band asks for three things a client can supply (date, place, rough brief) and promises a quote back in 24 hours, so contacting him is a small, bounded action. Two CTAs match how people actually enquire: WhatsApp for speed, form for detail. Dark band is the site's existing inversion pattern (see .invest on Services) — reused, not invented. M
08 Two-column `.about__fig` becomes the full-width band; remove object-fit: cover from it and use natural ratios with focal points. Per the no-cover rule .about__fig img currently inherits width:100%; height:auto plus an inline aspect-ratio, and the grid crops nothing — correct. The new band keeps the same guarantee: every <img> carries its true aspect-ratio and no object-fit, so a 2400×1866 landscape and a 1350×2400 vertical both render uncropped. Focal point, if ever needed, is object-position only. S
09 Bind layout to orientationchange; no resize listener anywhere on the route. iOS correctness, non-negotiable iOS fires resize on every address-bar collapse. A resize handler that repositions or recomputes anything produces a white flash and a jump to the top of the document. This page has no JS at all, which is the safest possible answer — pure CSS layout, one breakpoint, zero repaint logic. If a portrait is added later, render it via <img> dimensions rather than a computed box. S
10 Every price, term and delivery window on this page is sourced from SITE.pricing, not written fresh. Resolved during drafting, and it matters The first draft of this page invented a wedding package (R7,500) and a 14-day turnaround. Both contradicted config.ts — the real figures are the four packages above, 5 business days for Starter / Essential / Social and 7 for Signature, and there is no wedding offering at all. On a credibility page, one number that disagrees with the Services page costs more trust than a page that never gave a number. Build this by reading SITE.pricing into the markup, not by typing prices into copy: when a package price changes in config, About must change with it. S

Net effect. 1206px → roughly 2840px at 393px, and the added height is five new blocks of client-facing information rather than white space. Images drop from two to one by design: the page uses fewer photographs and says more with each one. Nothing here claims a studio, a second shooter, a client roster, or a turnaround he has not promised in config.ts — every claim on the page is one he can keep to on the day, which is the only credibility a one-person business has available. The single genuinely blocking item is change 01's photograph: twenty minutes of window light, shot once, and the largest credibility gap on the site closes permanently.
